I am a staff scientist working on the science development of the Wide Field Instrument (WFI) of the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope (Roman) within the Instruments Division at Space Telescope Science Institute (STScI).

I am from the Republic of Korea and came to the U.S. in 2009 to pursue a degree in Astronomy at the Pennsylvania State University. Since then, I've been living in the U.S. I majored in Astronomy & Astrophysics and Physics and minored in Mathematics in undergraduate. I worked as a post-bac research assistant in the Astronomy & Astrophysics department at Pennsylvania State University under the supervision of Prof. Jason Wright. I went to Boston in 2014 for graduate school where I got my Ph.D in Astronomy from Boson University in July of 2020 under the advise of Prof. Philip Muirhead. Then I was the Immersion GRating INfrared Spectrometer (IGRINS) postdoctoral fellow at the University of Texas at Austin. 

My research interest lies on low-mass stars (also known as M dwarf stars), stars that are cooler and smaller than our own Sun. These stars are the most common type of star in the Milky Way galaxy and they make up over 70% the population by number. M dwarfs are known to host exoplanets and in order to study those planets, we need to know the stars first. All my works involve characterizing M dwarf stellar properties including mass, radius, and magnetic field strenghts. All these properties are essential for characterzing the exoplanets and hence to answer whether or not the planets are habitable.
